What I work on

My work runs from atoms to patients. I have built predictors for drug toxicity and rare disease genetics, simulated how single mutations change a protein, analysed single-cell and bulk omics for target decisions, and shipped the pipelines and tools that let other scientists use the results.

Drug safety and ADMET prediction

Machine learning that predicts absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion and toxicity from structure. I co-developed ADMET-AI and led its cardiotoxicity extension, which outperformed common ADMET predictors on FDA drug-induced cardiotoxicity labels.

RDKit, scikit-learn, XGBoost, SHAP, hERG, BBB permeability

Generative and constrained molecular design

Design loops that generate synthesizable molecules from Enamine REAL building blocks with SyntheMol, then rank them by potency, BBB permeability and safety using QSAR and ADMET models in an active learning cycle.

SyntheMol, Enamine REAL, QSAR, MPO, active learning

Molecular simulation and structure

Molecular dynamics and free-energy analysis to explain how variants and ligands change protein behaviour, plus docking for off-target and selectivity profiling.

OpenMM, GROMACS, NAMD, AutoDock Vina

Human genetics and rare disease

Variant interpretation and gene-pair prediction for undiagnosed patients, built with clinicians in the Undiagnosed Diseases Network.

Random forests, GATK4, VEP, WGS

Multi-omics and target assessment

Bulk and single-cell RNA-seq, proteomics and phenotypic data from human iPSC models and patient tissue, used to judge whether a target is worth pursuing.

DESeq2, Scanpy, Seurat, pseudobulk

Research software and data platforms

Pipelines and tools that other scientists use directly: a SLURM whole-genome sequencing pipeline, Streamlit dashboards, interactive analysis consoles, and a tested, modular research platform that pairs LLMs with PubMed, GEO, ChEMBL, ClinVar and ClinicalTrials.gov.

Python, R, SQL, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Streamlit, HPC

Human-relevant models and wet-lab experience

I started at the bench with sarcomeric protein biochemistry for cardiomyopathy mutations, and more recently have cultured patient-derived iPSC neural progenitors and astrocytes. I also co-wrote a JCI review on moving safety testing beyond animal models.

iPSC culture, protein biochemistry, NAMs, FDA Modernization Act 2.0

Selected work

A closer look at a few projects: the problem, and what I did about it.

Circulation, 2025First author

Interpretable prediction of drug-induced cardiotoxicity

Cardiotoxicity is a common reason drugs fail late or are withdrawn. I led the conceptualization and deployment of a cardiotoxicity model built on ADMET-AI and trained on the FDA drug-induced cardiotoxicity (DICT) dataset. It outperformed common ADMET predictors, and an interpretability analysis shows which molecular properties drive each prediction.

The underlying platform, ADMET-AI (Bioinformatics, 2024), is among the fastest and most accurate free ADMET predictors and is used to triage large chemical libraries. It is a collaboration between Greenstone Biosciences and James Zou's group at Stanford.

Circulation paper ADMET-AI paper ADMET-AI code
AJHG, 2021First author

Finding digenic disease genes with machine learning

Some rare diseases are caused by variants in two genes acting together, which single-gene analysis misses. I built DiGePred, a random forest that predicts digenic disease gene pairs. With the Undiagnosed Diseases Network it was applied to more than 100 undiagnosed patients and several rare disease cohorts, and it contributed to identifying a new gene pair in Mayer-Rokitansky-Küster-Hauser syndrome.

AJHG paper MRKH follow-up
HGG Advances, 2022First author

Personalized structural biology for epilepsy variants

Two de novo variants two residues apart in the KCNC2 (Kv3.2) potassium channel caused different epilepsy phenotypes. Using molecular dynamics and free-energy analysis, I showed that V471L acts as gain of function and V469L as loss of function. Biochemistry and electrophysiology later confirmed both predictions.

HGG Advances paper
Greenstone BiosciencesOngoing

Synthesizable design under ADMET constraints

A closed-loop chemistry workflow. SyntheMol proposes molecules that can be made from Enamine REAL building blocks with reaction-aware rules, QSAR and ADMET models score them, and an active learning loop retrains on each round. Multi-parameter optimization balances potency against BBB permeability and hERG liability. I work with medicinal chemists and external CRO partners to take ranked compounds forward.

Cell 2024, Nature 2025Co-author

Cardiac fibrosis and human iPSC models

At the Stanford Cardiovascular Institute I build models of drug response and toxicity that combine whole-genome sequencing, transcriptomics and functional readouts from human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes, and I link those in vitro phenotypes to clinical endpoints. I am a co-author on the Wu lab's multiscale fibrosis screen that identified MD2 as a therapeutic target, and on the follow-up showing that blocking stromal mechanosensing suppresses cardiac fibrosis.

Cell paper Nature paper
