Research areas

My work runs from atoms to patients. I have built predictors for drug toxicity and rare disease genetics, simulated how single mutations change a protein, analysed single-cell and bulk omics for target decisions, and shipped the pipelines and tools that let other scientists use the results.

Drug safety and ADMET prediction

Machine learning that predicts absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion and toxicity from structure. I co-developed ADMET-AI and led its cardiotoxicity extension, which outperformed common ADMET predictors on FDA drug-induced cardiotoxicity labels.

RDKit, scikit-learn, XGBoost, SHAP, hERG, BBB permeability
  • Co-developed ADMET-AI (Bioinformatics 2024) with James Zou's group at Stanford, a free platform that predicts 41 ADMET properties fast enough to screen large chemical libraries.
  • Led ADMET-AI DICT (Circulation 2025). Trained an XGBoost classifier on ADMET-AI predictions for 555 drugs from the FDA DICTRank set, 293 with the most cardiotoxicity concern and 262 with none. It reached a ROC AUC of 0.72 against 0.67 for SwissADME.
  • Explained the model with SHAP. CYP2D6 substrate status, Nrf2 antioxidant response and heat shock factor response were the strongest drivers, and withdrawn drugs share the feature profile of the most-concern group.
  • Ran large-scale ADMET reports for medicinal chemistry, including a 2,156-compound combinatorial analogue series across four modification sites.
  • In development: a gradient-boosting BBB permeability model, and docking-based hERG and mutagenicity prediction.

Generative and constrained molecular design

Design loops that generate synthesizable molecules from Enamine REAL building blocks with SyntheMol, then rank them by potency, BBB permeability and safety using QSAR and ADMET models in an active learning cycle.

SyntheMol, Enamine REAL, QSAR, MPO, active learning
  • Built an end-to-end workflow in which SyntheMol proposes molecules from Enamine REAL building blocks under reaction-aware rules, so every candidate has a plausible synthesis route.
  • Scored candidates with QSAR and ADMET models, and used multi-parameter optimization to trade potency against BBB permeability and hERG liability.
  • Closed the loop with active learning: each round of generated hypotheses retrains the models before the next round is ranked.
  • Built RDKit pipelines for featurization, fingerprinting and SAR modeling, and worked with medicinal chemists and external CRO partners to take ranked compounds forward.

Molecular simulation and structure

Molecular dynamics and free-energy analysis of how variants change a protein and how drug candidates bind their targets, plus docking for off-target and selectivity profiling.

OpenMM, GROMACS, NAMD, AutoDock Vina
  • Ran the molecular dynamics for the Wu lab's cardiac fibrosis studies (Cell 2024, Nature 2025), simulating how drug candidates bind their protein targets. We validated that saracatinib binds Src kinase more strongly and stably than the other candidates tested.
  • Showed with MD and free-energy analysis that two neighbouring KCNC2 epilepsy variants act in opposite directions, gain and loss of function, before experiments confirmed it (HGG Advances 2022).
  • Analyse ligand RMSD, interaction energy, residue flexibility (RMSF) and pocket volume across replicate simulations to rank binders.
  • Dock against kinase structures with AutoDock Vina, with tautomer-aware ligand preparation, for off-target and selectivity profiling.

Circulation 2025 First author

Interpretable prediction of drug-induced cardiotoxicity

Cardiotoxicity is a common reason drugs fail late or are withdrawn. I led the conceptualization and deployment of a model built on ADMET-AI and trained on 555 drugs from the FDA DICTRank dataset, 293 with the most cardiotoxicity concern and 262 with none.

An XGBoost classifier on ADMET-AI predictions outperformed SwissADME (ROC AUC 0.72 against 0.67).

The underlying platform, ADMET-AI (Bioinformatics 2024), predicts 41 ADMET properties and is a collaboration between Greenstone Biosciences and James Zou's group at Stanford.

Nature 2025, Cell 2024 Co-author

How anti-fibrotic drug candidates bind their targets

The Wu lab at Stanford screened drugs against cardiac fibrosis in human cell and tissue models. In both studies I ran the molecular dynamics simulations showing how the drug candidates bind their protein targets.

In the Nature study a docking screen of 10,147 repurposing compounds was narrowed to five for simulation, and we validated that saracatinib binds strongly to Src kinase. It had the strongest interaction energy and the most stable pose of the five over 100 ns.

AJHG 2021 First author

Finding digenic disease genes with machine learning

Some rare diseases are caused by variants in two genes acting together, which single-gene analysis misses. I built DiGePred, a random forest that predicts digenic disease gene pairs, trained with 10-fold cross validation on a set where non-digenic pairs outnumber digenic ones about 75 to 1.

False positives were checked on gene pairs from 38 unaffected relatives and true positives on 13 new digenic pairs from the literature. With the Undiagnosed Diseases Network it was applied to 100+ undiagnosed patients, and it contributed to a new candidate gene pair for Mayer-Rokitansky-Küster-Hauser syndrome.

The features behind each prediction are biological rather than statistical: how close two genes sit in protein interaction and pathway networks, whether they are expressed in the same tissues, and how their evolutionary histories compare.

Across projects Ongoing

Finding targets in transcriptomic data

Most target work starts with a differential expression table. I run RNA-seq from public cohorts and from in-house experiments through DESeq2, then read the result as a volcano: how many genes move, how far, and how confidently.

Enrichment over GO terms and pathways then says what the moving genes have in common, and intersecting a disease signature with a compound's signature nominates both targets and repurposing candidates. The same workflow sits behind the pulmonary fibrosis and rheumatoid arthritis analyses described elsewhere on this page.

Computer vision Ongoing

Scoring cell state from images

Alexander disease is caused by mutations in GFAP, and patient astrocytes change shape: the cell body swells and the fine processes retract. Scoring that by eye across a plate is slow and depends on who is counting.

With Intel, I am training Intel Geti models on microscopy of patient-derived iPSC astrocytes. One segments nuclei, the other sorts whole cells into several morphological states rather than a single healthy or diseased call, giving a graded measure of how far a line has shifted and whether a treatment moves it back.

HGG Advances 2022 First author

Personalized structural biology for epilepsy variants

Two de novo variants two residues apart in the KCNC2 (Kv3.2) potassium channel caused different forms of developmental epileptic encephalopathy. Using molecular dynamics and free-energy analysis, I showed that V471L acts as gain of function and V469L as loss of function, because V469L constricts the pore and raises the barrier for potassium ions.

I led the investigation across clinicians, biochemists and electrophysiologists, and their experiments later confirmed both predictions.

iPSC-CM data analysis Ongoing

Calcium imaging analysis for iPSC cardiomyocytes

A Python pipeline for calcium optical imaging of iPSC-derived cardiomyocyte organoids and monolayers in 96-well plates. It segments each organoid, extracts its calcium transient, and measures beat rate, amplitude, transient duration (CaTD50, CaTD80, CaTD90), upstroke and decay kinetics, beat-to-beat variability and synchrony.

Drug responses are normalised to vehicle wells, so arrhythmia and line-to-line differences show up per plate. It reads two microscope formats, including 100 fps recordings with light-source flicker removed, and an interactive plate explorer and Streamlit app let biologists review every well.

The readouts are the ones that matter for cardiac safety. A hERG blocker such as dofetilide lengthens the calcium transient and pushes wells into irregular beating, and the pipeline reports that as a shift against the vehicle rows on the same plate.

AI-driven fibrosis drug discovery Ongoing

A human pan-fibrosis atlas for targets and drugs

Fibrosis drives organ failure in the lung, liver, kidney, skin, heart, gut and pancreas. I am analysing public human RNA-seq datasets organ by organ and pooling effect sizes across studies, to find the program the organs share and the protein targets behind it.

LINCS perturbation signatures and other approaches then rank drugs that reverse the fibrotic signature. Rankings will be benchmarked on known antifibrotics such as nintedanib and pirfenidone before they are trusted.

Stem Cell Research 2026 Co-author

Whole-genome QC for patient-derived iPSC lines

Before anyone builds a disease model on a new patient iPSC line, the line has to be shown to be the right donor and genomically intact. I built and run the genome analysis for the Stanford Cardiovascular Institute iPSC biobank: it confirms sample identity, checks sequencing quality, infers genetic ancestry, annotates pathogenic variants, and screens for copy number changes the cells picked up in culture.

That QC supports the lab's published iPSC line reports, including lines from Fabry disease patients carrying GLA mutations, from patients with thoracic aortic aneurysm carrying ACTA2 mutations, and from hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia patients carrying ACVRL1 mutations.

Kinase LLM Ongoing

An LLM research assistant for kinase inhibitor discovery

A locally hosted Qwen model answers kinase drug discovery questions from a human kinase knowledge graph. The graph joins the human kinome tree, measured binding from ChEMBL and BindingDB, profiling panels, structures, clinical evidence, trials, patents and literature.

The model acts as a Principal Scientist. It plans each question, sends it to specialist roles that each have their own tools, checks their evidence and writes an answer whose numbered citations open the source rows.

The next stage adds Enamine REAL space and multi-parameter optimization to propose new, selective kinase inhibitors that can actually be made.

Ongoing

Synthesizable design under ADMET constraints

A closed-loop chemistry workflow. SyntheMol proposes molecules that can be made from Enamine REAL building blocks with reaction-aware rules, so every candidate has a synthesis route. Boltz-2 predicts the complex and its binding affinity, molecular dynamics checks whether that complex holds together, and QSAR and ADMET models score potency, BBB permeability and hERG liability.

Multi-parameter optimization ranks what survives all of it. Medicinal chemists and external CRO partners then make and test the top candidates, and those results retrain the models before the next round. The aim is safe, active, synthesizable candidates for inflammatory and fibrotic diseases.
